# Evidence update v0.3 — new 2026 sources and Viet Nam correction

**Date checked:** 2026-11-02. This supplements, but does not rewrite, the earlier scan; use this as the current update.

## Verified updates

### Resistance-training dose, frequency and effort

- **ACSM 2026 full-text result:** the overview includes 137 systematic reviews in healthy adults. Higher weekly hypertrophy volume (including the ≥10-set dose-response signal) is associated with greater hypertrophy on average; it is not an individual minimum. For hypertrophy, its reviewed comparisons did not find consistent effects for load or failure; frequency was not influential when total volume was equated. The article reports insufficient evidence to determine the hypertrophy effect of inter-set rest and insufficient evidence to quantify exact RIR targets. These findings do not prove all methods equivalent. Full article sections inspected. [ACSM position stand](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12965823/).
- **Pelland et al. 2026:** meta-regressions of 67 studies and 2,058 participants find a positive weekly-volume relationship for hypertrophy and strength with diminishing returns. Hypertrophy's frequency slope is compatible with negligible effects; strength frequency may be beneficial with diminishing returns. Their fractional direct/indirect-set model cautions against treating every compound set as a full direct set for each muscle. PubMed abstract inspected; full article still required for a full methods/limitations audit. [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41343037/).
- **Refalo et al. 2023:** the full meta-analysis reports no evidence that momentary muscular failure is superior to non-failure for hypertrophy; available studies do not identify a precise optimal RIR. [Full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9935748/).

These sources reinforce exclusion of fixed MEV/MAV/MRV bands, universal failure/RIR targets, rigid frequency rules and exact per-session volume cutoffs. They also show why the ≥10-set result must be phrased as a population dose-response signal rather than a prescription.

### Vietnamese exercise evidence (correction to earlier scan)

The previous desk scan did not verify a Vietnamese gym-user or intervention report. A targeted follow-up found a directly relevant but narrow 2026 cluster-randomized trial: 90 community-dwelling adults aged 60–70 with AWGS-defined sarcopenia in Can Gio; 24 weeks of aerobic plus resistance exercise, supervised weekly during weeks 1–12 then more home-based. The exercise-only community showed better handgrip strength than control and modest muscle-mass change. **There was only one community per arm**, so treatment is confounded with community and the authors caution that definitive treatment effects cannot be determined; the nutrition contribution cannot be isolated. This is local evidence for older Vietnamese adults with sarcopenia, not for young recreational gym-goers, muscle-building volume rules, or ethnic differences in adaptation. [PubMed abstract and study metadata](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42797063/).

**Corrected statement:** Vietnam-specific exercise-intervention evidence does exist, including this 2026 study. No large, representative report of Vietnamese recreational gym users' needs or training outcomes was verified. The study must not be generalized into a uniform Vietnamese phenotype or an ethnicity-based training dose.
